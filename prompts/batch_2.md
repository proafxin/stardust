Several minor variations are known: in man https://www.britannica.com/topic/human-being , gestation for males is three to four days longer than that for females; and in cattle, bulls are carried about one day longer than heifers. In both species gestation of twins is five to six days less than for singlets. In animals such as the rabbit or pig, which bear many young at a time, gestation is shorter for larger litters than for smaller ones. Heredity also influences gestation; in cattle https://www.britannica.com/animal/cattle-livestock the mean gestation period for Holstein- Friesians https://www.britannica.com/animal/Holstein-Friesian is 279 days; for Brown Swiss https://www.britannica.com/animal/Brown-Swiss , 290 days; other breeds fall between these extremes. When hybrids https://www.britannica.com/science/hybrid are produced by crossing two species with different gestation periods, the hybrid https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/hybrid is carried for a period lying somewhere between those of the two parents and tending toward the mother’s species. Thus a mare carries a mule https://www.britannica.com/animal/mule-mammal foal (fathered by a jackass) about 10 days longer than the normal period for the horse (about 337 days).
